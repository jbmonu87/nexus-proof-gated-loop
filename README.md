# Nexus: Strategy to Execution With AI

**A private desktop workspace for business strategy · Unshipped R&D · Evidence reviewed October 15, 2026**

Nexus brings presentations, documents, and spreadsheets into one local-first workspace. I’m exploring how business teams could move from assumptions and analysis to a decision, with AI agents helping operate the software. Today, the most concrete product work is making those native editors useful and dependable; the wider connected-work and assistant experience remains incomplete.

I’m **JB Monu, a strategy and operations executive** with a consulting and transformation background. Nexus is my hands-on R&D project: I own the product vision, workflow design, requirements, agent operating model, acceptance standards, and final judgment. **AI coding agents implement most of the software.**

The business problem is familiar: assumptions live in a workbook, reasoning in a memo, and the recommendation in a deck. I want to test a more coherent way to work across them, while learning what it takes to direct AI implementation responsibly.

## What I contribute

The contribution is moving from an ambiguous priority to a usable capability: choosing the problem, defining the intended experience, coordinating implementation, and judging what the evidence supports. After I approve an outcome, AI orchestration handles routine routing and sequencing. Separate agents build and verify; I retain product decisions and final human judgment.

One standard makes that concrete: content an editor cannot render should still survive save. Complete preservation is a requirement I hold the project to, not a guarantee it has achieved. [My contribution and transferable practice](docs/CASE_STUDY.md).

## One visible loop: review changed the deliverable

A separate reviewer caught an incorrect financial label in the first showcase. The arithmetic deducted fixed costs, so “contribution margin” was the wrong term. The inputs and real Nexus captures were revised to use a defined operating-surplus measure.

The calculation stayed the same; the interpretation changed. I set the brief and review requirements; the separate reviewer found the error and the working agent made the corrections.
